Medkart Savings Journey — MVP mobile experience and interaction specification

Status: Draft for prototype testing and implementation feasibility review Related PRD: Medkart Savings Journey PRD v0.1 Platform: Authenticated mobile app; responsive principles apply MVP screens: Purchase history, detail, savings, issue, issue status and purchase claim Excluded: Family assignment, refill consent integration and reorder Version: 0.1 • 19 July 2026 Disclosure: Independent candidate exercise; not Medkart’s actual interface

Design intent: Make the customer’s store purchase understandable and correctable while keeping identity, savings methodology and unavailable states explicit.

Prototype boundary: The yellow “independent concept” strip and fictional-data labels are portfolio disclosures. They are not proposed production UI components.

1.1 Navigation rules

  • Purchases is a dedicated authenticated destination in bottom navigation or an equivalent discoverable app entry.
  • Back returns to the previous screen without changing the transaction or issue state.
  • Deep links must require authentication and re-check authorization before displaying purchase data.
  • A failed or ambiguous claim never redirects to purchase detail; it remains in safe verification/support states.
  • Issue submission protects the record when “not mine” is selected before presenting any further transaction detail.

1.2 Global state model

StateRequired behaviour
LoadingUse stable skeletons; do not briefly flash cached data belonging to another account.
EmptyExplain that only eligible linked store purchases appear; offer safe claim/help actions.
Partial dataShow available transaction facts; suppress unverified invoice or savings sections.
OfflineShow last-known non-sensitive shell only if policy allows; defer protected detail until authorization can be checked.
ErrorUse actionable retry/support copy without exposing identifiers or internal error detail.
RestrictedHide transaction detail while ownership, correction or policy review is active.

2. Shared components and content rules

ComponentSpecificationAccessibility / trust requirement
Purchase cardStore, date, invoice reference, total, status and optional savings summaryEntire card is one accessible action; status is not communicated by colour alone.
Status tagCompleted, adjusted, returned, cancelled, corrected or restrictedReadable text label; screen-reader announcement; approved state mapping.
MoneyIndian currency formatting with clear paid/comparator/savings labelsNever rely on typography alone to distinguish paid and saved.
Invoice actionView/download secured invoiceReauthorize; non-predictable reference; visible error and retry.
Savings summaryApproved comparator, paid amount, saving and method linkSuppress if calculation is unavailable; use “example” only in prototype.
Issue categoryNot mine, duplicate, amount/status, invoice unavailableAvoid medical free text; protect “not mine” records promptly.
Claim verificationInvoice reference, purchase date and approved verification challengeReveal no transaction item, total or medicine data before verified match.

2.1 Content principles

  • Use “Amount paid” instead of ambiguous “Price”.
  • Use “Comparable price” only after pricing/legal define the approved comparator.
  • Explain why savings are unavailable rather than displaying ₹0 saved.
  • Do not imply that purchase history proves medicine consumption or adherence.
  • Never ask customers to enter diagnosis, dosage or prescription detail in general support text fields.
  • Use plain status language and provide next action for restricted, adjusted or unavailable records.

3. Purchase history

Help an authenticated customer find eligible in-store purchase records and understand their status at a glance.

Entry: Purchases navigation, post-login deep link or successful claim.

Data: Store display name; date/time; masked or approved invoice reference; paid total; record status; optional approved savings summary.

Primary actions: Open purchase detail; filter by date/store/status; start “claim a purchase” when needed.

Required states: Loading, populated, empty, offline, partial data, restricted card, pagination/end of list.

PRD traceability: FR-03–FR-06, FR-10, FR-12; AC-01–AC-03, AC-10.

Analytics: purchase_history_viewed; purchase_card_selected; claim_purchase_started.

Notes: Cumulative savings is optional and must be suppressed until comparison coverage and methodology are approved.

4. Purchase detail

Present one authoritative transaction in a way that is understandable, downloadable and correctable.

Entry: Selection from S01 or successful verified claim.

Data: Store/date; record state; approved line display; quantity; totals; discounts; invoice action; optional savings module.

Primary actions: Open invoice; open savings explanation; report issue; return to history.

Required states: Loading, complete, partial data, invoice unavailable, savings unavailable, adjusted/returned/cancelled, restricted.

PRD traceability: FR-07–FR-12; AC-04–AC-10.

Analytics: purchase_detail_viewed; invoice_opened; savings_explanation_opened; purchase_issue_started.

Notes: The retail system remains authoritative for totals and status. The app must label stale/unavailable states rather than infer values.

Figure 4. Independent MVP wireframe using fictional transaction data.

5. Savings explanation

Explain the comparison method so a customer can understand and trust a displayed savings amount.

Entry: Savings module on S02.

Data: Paid amount; approved comparator; saving; method name/version; eligible scope; limitation/unavailable explanation.

Primary actions: Return to purchase detail; optionally open approved methodology/help.

Required states: Available, partially comparable, unavailable, method updated, calculation under review.

PRD traceability: FR-09; BR-05; AC-05–AC-06.

Analytics: savings_explanation_viewed; savings_method_help_opened.

Notes: The interface cannot define the comparator. Pricing and legal owners must approve calculation rules before implementation.

Figure 5. Independent MVP wireframe using fictional transaction data.

6. Report a purchase issue

Give customers a safe, low-friction path to report ownership, duplication, amount/status or invoice problems.

Entry: “Something is wrong” on S02.

Data: Transaction reference passed securely; selected issue category; optional minimal note; authenticated account.

Primary actions: Select category; enter optional note; submit; cancel/back.

Required states: Default, selected, validation error, submitting, duplicate case, service unavailable.

PRD traceability: FR-11–FR-13; AC-08, AC-10.

Analytics: purchase_issue_started; issue_category_selected; purchase_issue_submitted; purchase_issue_failed.

Notes: For “not mine,” protect or restrict the record immediately according to policy. Free text must warn against unnecessary medical information.

Figure 6. Independent MVP wireframe using fictional transaction data.

7. Protected issue status

Confirm that the report was received and explain what happens next without continuing to expose disputed purchase details.

Entry: Successful S04 submission.

Data: Case reference; issue category; safe status; next-step copy; support route.

Primary actions: Return to history; contact support; later view case status if supported.

Required states: Received, reviewing, resolved, more verification required, unavailable.

PRD traceability: FR-10–FR-13; AC-07–AC-08.

Analytics: issue_status_viewed; support_contact_started.

Notes: Do not promise a resolution time until support ownership and SLA are approved. Avoid showing disputed medicine/item details.

Figure 7. Independent MVP wireframe using fictional transaction data.

8. Claim unmatched purchase

Allow a customer to verify a purchase that was not linked automatically without revealing transaction details before identity is established.

Entry: Empty/history help action or privacy-safe invitation.

Data: Invoice reference; purchase date; approved verification challenge; match outcome.

Primary actions: Verify and claim; request help; cancel.

Required states: Default, invalid input, no match, ambiguous match, challenge expired, too many attempts, verified, support required.

PRD traceability: FR-05, FR-12, FR-14; AC-03, AC-10.

Analytics: claim_purchase_started; claim_verification_submitted; claim_match_outcome; claim_support_started.

Notes: The wireframe uses a verification-code placeholder. Identity/privacy owners must select the actual challenge and anti-abuse controls.

11. Accessibility and responsive requirements

  • Minimum touch target follows the product design system; interactive cards expose one clear accessible name.
  • Text supports device scaling without clipping, overlap or loss of actions.
  • Status, savings and errors use text and icons in addition to colour.
  • Focus order follows visual order; focus returns predictably after modal or error dismissal.
  • Currency and dates are announced with meaningful labels, not as isolated numbers.
  • Forms provide visible labels, field-level errors and a summary when submission fails.
  • Reduced-motion preference is respected; no essential information depends on animation.
  • On wider screens, content may expand but sensitive data and action hierarchy remain unchanged.

12. Privacy, security and analytics constraints

AreaRequirement
AuthorizationRe-check authorization for history, detail, invoice, correction and claim endpoints; do not trust navigation state.
CachingDefine whether protected data may be cached; prevent account-switch leakage.
IdentifiersUse non-predictable references and approved masking in customer-visible UI.
Free textDo not send general issue notes into analytics; warn against medical detail.
AnalyticsPrefer status/category/method version and latency. Exclude item names, prescription images, diagnosis and unapproved identifiers.
Screenshots/supportSupport tools must minimize exposure and follow role-based access.
Deletion/retentionApply approved policy to links, issue records, analytics and audit data.

13. Design and engineering handoff checklist

  • Final design-system components and production brand tokens applied.
  • All screen states designed, including empty, error, restricted, adjusted and unavailable.
  • Identity, eligibility, savings and state-transition rules linked to implementation tickets.
  • API contracts define idempotency, authorization, status updates and error taxonomy.
  • Production copy reviewed by product, operations, support, pricing/legal and privacy/compliance.
  • Analytics schema reviewed for data minimization.
  • Accessibility annotations and test cases included.
  • Prototype tested with customers and store/support users before build approval.
  • Feature flag, kill switch, dashboards and incident owners documented.
