1. Current-state workflow

The current-state workflow captures only what was observed or recalled. It does not attempt to reproduce Medkart’s proprietary retail software or infer undocumented automation.

Figure 1 — Reconstructed store purchase workflow (Amber steps require verification)

Customer request → Inventory search → Item selection → Discount & membership → Bill completion → Receipt delivery (requires verification) → CRM / follow-up context (requires verification)

Important: No major usability problem or feature request was identified by the two experienced representatives.

1.2 Current-state unknowns

  • Exact membership duration, eligibility and discount rules.
  • Whether and how a digital bill is delivered.
  • How CRM medicine-completion timing is calculated and used.
  • Where reminder consent is collected, stored, changed and withdrawn.
  • How shared, recycled or incorrect phone numbers are handled.
  • Refund, return, cancellation and partial-fulfilment flows.

2. Proposed-state workflow

The future-state workflow starts only after a pharmacist-verified store transaction. Its central design principle is fail-safe identity matching: an uncertain match must never expose a customer’s purchase to another account.

Figure 2 — Proposed store-to-app workflow with match, consent and exception paths

Verified store transaction
        ↓
   Account match (decision)
   ├── no/uncertain → Unmatched or ambiguous (exception)
   │                       → offer safe claim/support path with verification
   │                       → after resolution → Customer actions
   └── yes → Eligible matched account
                → Digital purchase record
                → Optional organization (family assignment, separate opt-in)
                → Optional communication (reminder preference, separate opt-in)
                → Customer actions

5. Workflow acceptance gates

  • No store checkout dependency: app linking failure must not block a valid in-store sale.
  • No uncertain exposure: ambiguous account matches produce no customer-visible item data.
  • No bundled consent: purchase visibility, family assignment and reminder communication are separate decisions.
  • No unsupported savings claim: comparison method and source must be explainable and auditable.
  • No silent correction: refunds, returns, duplicates and ownership corrections update visible state and audit history.
  • No clinical inference: the workflow does not provide diagnosis, dosage or adherence advice.
