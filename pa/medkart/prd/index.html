Medkart Savings Journey

Connecting eligible in-store purchases to the customer app

Document status: Draft for discovery and concept testing — not approved for implementation Release scope: MVP: linked purchase record, digital invoice, explainable savings and correction handling Product surface: Customer app supported by retail transaction, identity and support workflows Primary user: Authenticated Medkart customer completing an eligible in-store purchase Owner: Candidate-created independent product exercise Version: 0.1 • 19 July 2026 Dependencies: Capability audit, customer research, identity review, privacy/compliance review and store pilot

Problem hypothesis: An in-store customer may benefit from a safe digital record of the purchase, invoice and savings. The need and the absence of an existing internal capability must both be validated before build approval.

MVP principle: Prove reliable transaction-to-account linking and customer value before adding family assignment, refill preferences or reorder. Any ambiguous match must fail closed and reveal no medicine information.

1. Background

An exploratory store study was conducted across two Medkart locations through approximately five informal interactions with two experienced store representatives. The walkthrough covered billing, inventory lookup, membership identification using a contact number, CRM context and reporting. Representatives appeared comfortable with the existing process and did not identify an immediate feature request.

Medkart’s public app listing already describes prescription upload, branded-versus-generic comparison, order tracking, refill reminders, family profiles and store discovery. The proposed opportunity is therefore not a standalone reminder feature. It explores whether an eligible in-store transaction can continue safely into the customer’s existing digital relationship.

2. Problem and opportunity

2.1 Problem hypothesis

After an in-store purchase, customers may lack a durable, app-accessible record that connects the bill, purchase information and an explainable savings summary. The impact, frequency and current workarounds have not yet been measured.

2.2 Opportunity statement

How might Medkart connect an eligible in-store transaction to the correct authenticated customer so they can retain the invoice, understand savings and resolve errors—without slowing store checkout or exposing sensitive purchase data?

2.3 Why this is worth testing

  • Makes the value of membership and generic-medicine savings more tangible after checkout.
  • May reduce friction when customers need an earlier bill or purchase detail.
  • Creates a foundation for later family, reminder and reorder journeys without bundling them prematurely.
  • Tests a measurable store-to-app handoff while respecting identity, privacy and operational constraints.

3.1 Product principles

  • Fail closed: uncertain identity means no customer-visible purchase details.
  • Checkout independence: app-linking failure never blocks a valid in-store sale.
  • Explain, do not imply: show how savings are calculated and when they are unavailable.
  • Separate purposes: purchase visibility is not reminder consent or family authorization.
  • Correctability: customers can report wrong, duplicate or outdated records through a visible path.
  • Minimum necessary data: analytics and support tools avoid unnecessary medicine or prescription detail.

4. Users and jobs to be done

UserJob to be done
In-store customerWhen I complete a store purchase, help me retain an accurate record so I can find the bill and understand what I paid later.
Customer managing household purchasesWhen I buy for more than one person, help me organize records safely without exposing them to the wrong person.
Store representativeWhen I finish checkout, let the digital handoff happen without adding complex steps or making me troubleshoot the app.
Support agentWhen a customer reports a wrong or duplicate record, give me a traceable way to verify and correct it without exposing more data.
Product / operationsWhen piloting the journey, show whether it provides value without increasing privacy incidents or store effort.

5. Scope and prioritization

PriorityCapabilities
MVP / MustEligible transaction ingestion; safe account matching; purchase list and detail; digital invoice; approved savings explanation; correction path; refund/cancellation state; audit and monitoring
Pilot / ShouldVerified claim flow for unmatched transactions; store locator/support handoff; lightweight customer education
Later / CouldAuthorized family assignment; explicit refill preference integration; reorder from purchase; cumulative savings dashboard
Not plannedClinical recommendations; automatic substitution; adherence scoring; full loyalty redesign; blocking checkout on app failure

5.1 Prioritization rationale

The MVP focuses on the smallest complete trust loop: create the right record, show it to the right person, explain it accurately and correct it when something goes wrong. Family assignment, refill integration and reorder increase authorization, consent and clinical-context complexity; they should follow only after the core link is safe and useful.

8. Business rules

IDRule
BR-01A transaction is visible only when source status, eligibility and account match all pass.
BR-02A phone number alone must not be assumed sufficient when shared, recycled, changed or otherwise ambiguous.
BR-03The customer app is not the authoritative source for transaction totals; it reflects approved retail-system state.
BR-04Purchase visibility does not constitute consent for refill, promotional or other communication.
BR-05Savings appear only when the comparator, calculation method and source meet approved rules.
BR-06Refunds, returns, cancellations and corrections update the visible record without deleting the required audit trail.
BR-07An account-linking or app outage cannot prevent store billing from completing.
BR-08Analytics must not contain prescription images, diagnosis, free-text medical notes or item names unless explicitly approved and necessary.

10. Non-functional requirements

IDAreaRequirement
NFR-01PrivacyMinimize data; apply purpose limitation, retention and deletion rules approved by privacy/compliance.
NFR-02SecurityEncrypt in transit/at rest as applicable; authorize every request; prevent predictable invoice references.
NFR-03ReliabilityTransaction ingestion is idempotent and retry-safe; failures are observable and recoverable.
NFR-04PerformancePilot targets must be set after baselining; app history should not materially slow primary app navigation.
NFR-05Checkout isolationNo downstream app, identity or analytics dependency may block retail checkout.
NFR-06AccessibilitySupport readable contrast, scalable text, screen-reader labels, focus order and non-colour status cues.
NFR-07LocalizationCustomer copy and savings explanations must support required languages and currency formatting.
NFR-08ObservabilityMonitor ingestion failures, match outcomes, visibility changes, correction volume and access anomalies.

11. Acceptance criteria

IDAcceptance criterion
AC-01Given a completed eligible transaction with a verified account match, when ingestion succeeds, then one purchase record appears for that authenticated customer.
AC-02Given the same transaction event is delivered more than once, when processed, then only one customer-visible record exists.
AC-03Given an ambiguous or conflicting account match, when ingestion is evaluated, then no item, amount or invoice detail is exposed.
AC-04Given an eligible record, when the customer opens detail, then store, date, approved line details, totals, status and invoice action are displayed.
AC-05Given an approved savings calculation, when shown, then the comparator and method are accessible in plain language.
AC-06Given savings inputs are unavailable or unapproved, when detail loads, then no unsupported savings amount is shown.
AC-07Given a return, refund or cancellation update, when the source event is processed, then the app record reflects the new state and preserves traceability.
AC-08Given a customer selects ‘This is not mine’, when submitted, then the detail is protected, a case is created and no additional data is exposed during verification.
AC-09Given the app-linking service is unavailable, when store checkout completes, then billing remains successful and the event can be retried asynchronously.
AC-10Given an unauthorized or unauthenticated request, when any purchase endpoint is called, then access is denied and the attempt is logged according to policy.

13. Rollout and release plan

StageScopeExit gate
Gate 0 — ValidateCustomer/store research; capability audit; identity, savings and privacy decisionsProblem, existing capability and owners confirmed
Gate 1 — DesignWireframes, threat review, event contract, support and store SOPAcceptance criteria and exception ownership approved
Gate 2 — Internal testSynthetic transactions and authorized internal accountsNo cross-account exposure; retry and correction paths pass

Gate 4 — ExpandIncremental stores and cohorts with monitoringPilot decision criteria met; unresolved risks accepted by owners

13.1 Rollback triggers

  • Any confirmed cross-account purchase disclosure.
  • Systematic incorrect totals, invoice access or savings calculation.
  • Store checkout degradation caused by the integration.
  • Material spike in duplicate or stale records beyond pilot tolerance.
  • Inability to stop visibility or communication after correction/withdrawal.

17. Definition of ready

  • Customer and store research has tested the problem and documented contradicting evidence.
  • Existing internal capabilities and duplicate scope have been reviewed.
  • MVP scope, workflows, wireframes and acceptance criteria are approved by relevant owners.
  • Identity, privacy, savings and transaction-eligibility rules are documented.
  • Event contract, source-of-truth ownership and reconciliation approach are agreed.
  • Support SOP, incident path, feature flag and rollback mechanism are ready.
  • Pilot metrics and thresholds are based on baseline data rather than invented targets.

18. Definition of done

  • All MVP functional and non-functional acceptance criteria pass.
  • Security, privacy, accessibility and operations reviews are complete.
  • Synthetic and authorized end-to-end tests cover happy paths and exceptions.
  • Monitoring, dashboards, alerts and audit logs are operational.
  • Store and support training artifacts are published and acknowledged.
  • Pilot release is feature-flagged, reversible and has named incident owners.
  • Post-pilot findings and proceed/refine/stop decision are documented.
