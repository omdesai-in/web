Standard Operating Procedure

Linked Purchase Support SOP

Store and customer-support handling for the proposed Savings Journey MVP

VERSION0.1 • 19 July 2026
OWNERProposed: Product Operations

Purpose: Provide consistent, privacy-safe handling when a customer asks about a linked store purchase, invoice, savings amount or incorrect record.

Disclosure: Proposed portfolio operating model based on the Medkart Savings Journey case study. This is not Medkart’s current SOP, policy or service commitment.

1. Operating principles

  • Store checkout is never delayed because app linking or support tools are unavailable.
  • Verify the customer through an approved method before discussing transaction detail. A phone number alone is not proof of ownership.
  • Reveal the minimum information necessary; never read out another account’s purchase details.
  • Do not interpret medicines, dosage or clinical suitability. Refer clinical questions to an authorized pharmacist/appropriate channel.
  • Do not promise savings, correction or resolution timelines unless the approved policy provides one.
  • When ownership is disputed, protect the record first and investigate second.

2. Standard customer-help flow

  1. Listen and classify — Ask what the customer is trying to do: find a purchase, open an invoice, understand savings, report incorrect details or report “not mine.” Do not ask for diagnosis, dosage or medicine history.
  2. Confirm channel and authorization — Use only the approved verification flow. If authorization is uncertain or the match is ambiguous, show/discuss no transaction detail and move to protected support.
  3. Use the correct path — Follow the scenario table below. Keep the retail billing source authoritative for transaction amount and lifecycle state.
  4. Create a case when required — Record case ID, transaction reference, category, safe description, timestamps and owner. Avoid customer contact details or medicine text in general notes unless policy requires and protects it.
  5. Confirm the next step — Give a case reference and safe status. Do not promise a date unless an approved SLA exists.
  6. Close with evidence — Record outcome, correction/reconciliation evidence and customer-safe closure reason. Link any product defect to the relevant QA case.

3.1 Mandatory P0 actions

  • Stop sharing or displaying the disputed record; do not ask the customer to reproduce exposure publicly.
  • Capture only safe identifiers, time, channel and affected feature; preserve technical logs through approved access.
  • Notify the named incident owners and pilot/release decision owner.
  • Evaluate feature flag containment and whether other records may be affected.
  • Do not close until protection, scope assessment, correction and communication decisions are recorded.

4. Data handling and closeout

4.1 Collect only what is necessary

Allowed when neededAvoid in general notes/analytics
Case ID, safe transaction reference, issue category, timestamps, state and owner.Full phone number, medicine names, prescription images, diagnosis, dosage or emotional/medical narrative.

4.2 Closure checklist

  • Customer-visible status is accurate and safe.
  • Record ownership/access is corrected or remains protected according to policy.
  • Totals, lifecycle state and savings are reconciled where applicable.
  • Root cause or product defect is linked, with regression scope.
  • Customer receives an approved closure explanation and support route.
  • Case retention and deletion follow approved policy.

5. Store briefing checklist

  • Know where the customer can find Purchases, invoice, savings explanation and Report issue.
  • Know that checkout continues during linking failure.
  • Know that phone-number presence does not authorize disclosure.
  • Know the “not mine” containment and escalation route.
  • Know the approved non-clinical boundaries and pharmacist referral route.
  • Have current support contacts and feature/pilot status.

Success measure: The customer receives a clear next step, the record stays protected, checkout remains unaffected and every material issue has an owner and traceable evidence.

1.1 Decision rules

  • If identity is uncertain: fail closed.
  • If savings cannot be verified: suppress the number.
  • If state conflicts with the retail source: show a safe pending/unavailable state and reconcile.
  • If linking is unavailable: checkout continues and recovery is asynchronous.
  • If another customer’s data may be exposed: protect first, activate the security/privacy path and assess scope.

3. Release process

  1. Scope lock — Confirm MVP, explicit exclusions, acceptance criteria, user groups and pilot boundary.
  2. Readiness review — Check the 17 release controls across Product, QA, Engineering, Privacy/Security, Data, Accessibility, Support and Operations.
  3. Evidence review — All P0 tests pass; no unresolved critical/high privacy or security defect; savings and lifecycle reconciliation are verified.
  4. Pilot approval — Record go/no-go participants, open risks, risk acceptances, feature-flag configuration and rollback owner.
  5. Controlled enablement — Enable the smallest approved cohort; confirm monitoring and support channels before expansion.
  6. Daily pilot review — Review privacy, identity match, duplicates, reconciliation, invoice, savings and issue metrics plus qualitative feedback.
  7. Scale, hold or rollback — Expand only after stable evidence and explicit approval; otherwise hold or execute rollback.

4.2 Rollback procedure

  1. Declare — Record trigger, scope, decision owner and time.
  2. Contain — Disable the feature or affected component through the approved feature flag/kill switch.
  3. Confirm store continuity — Verify checkout and core retail operations remain functional.
  4. Protect and reconcile — Protect disputed records; preserve audit data; define treatment for queued events and linked records.
  5. Communicate and recover — Notify relevant owners and pilot teams using approved copy. Fix the trigger, run regression, repeat readiness review and require explicit re-enable approval.

5.1 Weekly review agenda

  • Outcome and safety indicators versus expected range.
  • New P0/P1 issues, blocked test cases and unresolved root causes.
  • Customer and store feedback themes, separated from single anecdotes.
  • Decisions made, owners, dates and dependencies.
  • Changes required in PRD, feature spec, test coverage, SOP or user guide.
  • Recommendation: continue, hold, expand, narrow or rollback.

Your Medkart Store Purchases

How to find an eligible purchase, view its invoice, understand savings and report an issue

Purpose: Explain the proposed feature in simple language while setting honest expectations about linking, savings and support.

Disclosure: Proposed portfolio operating model based on the Medkart Savings Journey case study. This is not Medkart’s current SOP, policy or service commitment.

1. Find a store purchase

Screen not yet designed — described here instead: Mobile screen showing the “Store purchases” list view with an example savings banner (₹1,260) and three recent purchases (Medkart • University Road, Medkart • Central Avenue, Medkart • University Road) each with amount, status badge, and example saving.

1. Sign in Use the account connected through the approved Medkart verification process.

2. Open Purchases Eligible linked store purchases appear newest first.

3. Choose a purchase Check the store, date, amount and status before opening it.

4. If nothing appears Use Claim a purchase or contact support. No purchase detail is shown until verification succeeds.

Important: Not every store transaction may be eligible or linked immediately. A missing record does not mean your printed store bill is invalid.

What the status can mean

StatusMeaning
CompletedThe linked purchase is currently recorded as completed.
AdjustedThe amount or item state changed after the original bill.
ReturnedA return/refund update has been recorded.
ProtectedDetails are hidden while ownership or an issue is reviewed.
UnavailableSome information cannot be shown safely or reliably right now.

2. View details and your invoice

Screen not yet designed — described here instead: Mobile “Purchase details” screen showing store name, date, status badge, amount paid (₹640), an example saving callout (₹540) with a “How this is calculated” link, an itemized purchase summary (Medicine A, Medicine B, Health product C, subtotal, store discount, amount paid), a “View digital invoice” button, and a “Something is wrong” button.

Purchase details Review the store, date, line items, quantity, amount, discount and current status when available.

View invoice Open or download the secured digital invoice. If it is unavailable, retry or use the support route.

Amount paid This should match the authoritative transaction after returns or adjustments.

Something is wrong Use the issue action instead of sharing transaction screenshots publicly.

If the invoice does not open

  • Check your connection and retry once.
  • Keep your printed invoice if you received one at the store.
  • Use Report issue or the approved support channel if the problem continues.
  • Never send passwords, OTPs or payment credentials to obtain an invoice.

3. Understand a displayed saving

Screen not yet designed — described here instead: Mobile “How savings are calculated” screen showing an example comparison of Approved comparable price (₹1,180) vs Amount paid (₹640), the resulting example saving (₹540), an explanation panel (Approved comparator, Same purchase context, Not always available, Method version), and a disclaimer that prototype values/methodology are illustrative.

Amount paid The amount recorded for the purchase after applicable discounts and adjustments.

Comparable price An approved reference used for the explanation—not an arbitrary estimate.

You saved The difference calculated using the stated method and eligible items.

Unavailable If a reliable comparator is missing, no savings amount should be invented. The app should explain that it is unavailable.

Please note: A linked purchase or savings display does not provide medical advice and does not prove that medicine was consumed. Ask an authorized healthcare professional for clinical guidance.

4. Report a problem safely

Screen not yet designed — described here instead: Mobile “Report a purchase issue” screen with a warning banner (“Do not include prescription, diagnosis or unnecessary medical details”), four selectable issue options (This purchase is not mine, I see a duplicate purchase, Amount or status looks incorrect, I cannot open the invoice), an optional note field, and a “Submit issue” button.

1. Select the issue Choose Not mine, duplicate, incorrect amount/status or invoice unavailable.

2. Keep notes minimal Do not enter diagnosis, dosage, prescription details, passwords or OTPs.

3. Submit You should receive a case reference and safe status.

4. For “not mine” The disputed record should be protected while it is reviewed.

When to contact support

  • You can see a purchase that is not yours.
  • A purchase remains duplicated or shows the wrong return/refund state.
  • The invoice or savings explanation appears inconsistent.
  • A claim repeatedly fails or the app asks for unsafe information.

5. Claim a missing purchase

Screen not yet designed — described here instead: Mobile “Claim a store purchase” screen with a privacy-first claim banner, input fields for invoice number, purchase date, and verification code, a “Verify and claim” button, an “I need help” button, and a note that the screen intentionally reveals no transaction or medicine detail before verification.

Start safely Enter only the approved invoice reference/date and complete the approved verification challenge.

No detail before verification The app should not reveal items, amounts or medicine information while the match is uncertain.

No match or ambiguous match Use the support route. Repeated guessing may be limited to protect customers.

Successful verification The purchase can be added to history only after ownership is confirmed.

6. Privacy and safety reminders

  • Use your own account and a trusted device. Sign out on shared devices.
  • Do not share purchase screenshots publicly.
  • Medkart support should never ask for your password or OTP.
  • A purchase does not automatically provide consent for refill reminders.
  • For urgent medical needs, use the appropriate clinical/emergency channel—not a product support case.
