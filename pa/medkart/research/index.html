Independent research plan • Do not collect medical details unnecessary for the study

Purpose: Validate the problem before approving a product requirement Participants: Customers, store representatives, area managers and internal stakeholders Format: Moderated interviews; concept shown only after current-behaviour questions Version: 0.1 • 19 July 2026 Status: Proposed research plan — not yet conducted

Research rule: Do not begin by pitching the feature. First understand current behaviour, workarounds, trust, consent and failure cases. Show the concept only after the participant has described their existing experience.

1. Research objectives

  • Understand what customers do with pharmacy bills and purchase information after an in-store transaction.
  • Test whether customers understand Medkart membership and the savings they receive.
  • Learn whether a store purchase inside the app would solve a meaningful customer task.
  • Identify store exceptions in membership lookup, billing, CRM and customer communication.
  • Understand consent, family-account, privacy and account-matching requirements.
  • Evaluate the proposed concept without treating positive reactions as proof of demand.

4. Moderator protocol

4.1 Opening and consent script

Read aloud: “Thank you for speaking with me. I am exploring how customers and store teams handle purchase information, bills, membership and follow-up after an in-store medicine purchase. This is an independent product exercise, not official Medkart research. There are no right answers. You may skip any question or stop at any time. Please do not share prescription details, diagnoses or other medical information that is not necessary for this conversation. May I take anonymized notes?“

4.2 Interview behaviour

  • Ask for the last real example before asking what someone would prefer.
  • Use neutral prompts such as “Tell me more” and “What happened next?”
  • Do not defend Medkart, explain the proposed design or teach the participant how to answer.
  • Separate observed behaviour, participant interpretation and moderator inference in notes.
  • Do not collect names, phone numbers, prescription images or medicine details in the portfolio artifact.
  • Ask permission before quoting; anonymize role and location in public outputs.

5. Customer interview guide

5.1 Context and recent behaviour

  1. Tell me about the last time you bought medicine from a physical pharmacy. Optional probes: Who was it for? How did you choose the store? What happened from arrival to payment?
  2. What information did you need during or after that purchase? Optional probes: Price, substitute explanation, availability, bill, dosage instructions, next purchase, reimbursement
  3. What did you do with the bill after leaving the store? Optional probes: Kept it, photographed it, discarded it, used it for reimbursement, needed it later
  4. Have you ever needed to find an old pharmacy purchase or bill? Tell me about the most recent time. Optional probes: What triggered it? How long did it take? What happened if it was unavailable?
  5. When purchasing for family members, how do you remember which purchase belongs to whom? Optional probes: Shared phone number, paper bag, prescription, memory, messaging

5.2 Membership and savings

  1. Are you a member of any pharmacy programme? What do you believe the membership provides? Optional probes: Fee, duration, discount, renewal, restrictions
  2. How do you decide whether the discount or generic alternative is valuable? Optional probes: Comparison point, trust, pharmacist explanation, total bill
  3. After paying, do you know how much you saved? How confident are you in that figure? Optional probes: What would make the calculation trustworthy?
  4. Would seeing savings across several purchases help you do anything differently? Optional probes: Avoid “Would you like it?”; ask for the task and decision it would support

5.3 Digital behaviour and communication

  1. Which pharmacy or healthcare apps do you currently use, if any? Optional probes: Ordering, reminders, records, family use, store locator, support
  2. How would you expect an in-store purchase to appear in an app? Optional probes: Automatic, claim with bill number, OTP, not at all
  3. How should a pharmacy ask permission before sending refill communication? Optional probes: Channel, frequency, timing, pause, opt-out
  4. What would make you uncomfortable about linking purchases to an app or family profile? Optional probes: Shared phone, sensitive item, recycled number, device access, deletion

5.4 Concept feedback — show only now

Neutral concept: “Imagine that after an eligible in-store purchase, a pharmacist-verified transaction could appear in your Medkart account. It might include the bill, amount paid, an explanation of savings, optional family assignment and an optional refill preference. Nothing would be shared or messaged without the required account match and consent.”

  1. What is your first reaction to this concept? Optional probes: What is useful, unnecessary, unclear or concerning?
  2. Which part, if any, would help with something you already do? Optional probes: Ask for a real task, not a rating
  3. What information should never appear automatically? Optional probes: Visibility, family access, sensitive purchases
  4. What would you expect to happen if the wrong purchase appeared? Optional probes: Correction, support, audit, deletion
  5. If only one part could be tested first, which would you choose and why? Optional probes: Digital bill, purchase history, savings, family assignment, reminder preference

6. Store representative interview guide

  1. Please walk me through the most recent customer bill from search to receipt. Optional probes: Membership lookup, discount explanation, substitute discussion, payment, bill delivery
  2. What happens when the phone number is missing, shared, incorrect or already linked to another person? Optional probes: How often? Who resolves it?
  3. How do you explain the membership fee, duration and additional discount? Optional probes: Which questions or misunderstandings repeat?
  4. Which receipts or messages are produced after billing? Optional probes: Printed bill, SMS, WhatsApp, email, app; verify rather than assume
  5. What customer information is visible in CRM, and what actions can you take from it? Optional probes: Expected medicine completion, contact, reminder, permission, escalation
  6. Which checkout situations take longer than expected? Optional probes: Membership, unavailable medicine, substitution, price explanation, system failure
  7. How do customers ask about earlier purchases or savings? Optional probes: Frequency, current response, access limits
  8. If a store bill appeared in the app, what work would this add or remove for you? Optional probes: Checkout time, correction, training, support ownership
  9. What failures would make this feature unsafe or difficult to operate? Optional probes: Wrong account, duplicate bill, refund, return, partial fulfilment, shared phone
  10. What would you need in an SOP before using this workflow? Optional probes: Eligibility, consent, exception handling, escalation, customer explanation

7. Area manager interview guide

  1. Which store workflows do you review during visits, and what indicates that they are working well?
  2. Where do membership, billing, CRM or customer-communication practices vary across stores?
  3. Which customer complaints or questions repeat across locations?
  4. How are representatives trained when a workflow or software feature changes?
  5. What operational metrics matter for checkout, membership and repeat purchase?
  6. What risks would you expect from linking store transactions to customer accounts?
  7. What should be tested in one or two stores before a broader rollout?
  8. What evidence would convince you to continue, change or stop the pilot?

9. Prototype feedback tasks

Use these only after an initial concept has been converted into wireframes. Observe behaviour before asking for opinions.

  • Find the purchase made at a physical store yesterday.
  • Explain how much was paid and how the displayed savings were calculated.
  • Assign the purchase to an authorized family profile.
  • Choose a reminder channel, then change or withdraw that preference.
  • Report that a transaction does not belong to you.
  • Find a digital invoice and contact the correct support route.

11. Synthesis and decision framework

  • Group notes by behaviour or task, not by whether a participant said they liked the feature.
  • Record the number of participants who independently described the same behaviour, while keeping qualitative context.
  • Separate customer desirability, operational viability, technical feasibility and privacy/compliance evidence.
  • Actively document disconfirming evidence and existing capabilities that make the proposal unnecessary.
  • Do not convert a small directional sample into percentages representing all Medkart customers.
DecisionConditionNext action
ProceedRepeated real task + acceptable trust model + feasible matching + manageable store impactDraft PRD and prototype pilot
RefineValue exists but scope, consent, calculation or workflow is unclearNarrow concept and run targeted validation
StopNo meaningful task, existing solution is sufficient, or risks outweigh likely valueDocument learning; do not build

12. Research integrity checklist

  • Participant understands this is an independent exercise, not official Medkart research.
  • Note-taking permission recorded.
  • No unnecessary medical, prescription or directly identifying data collected.
  • Current behaviour explored before showing the concept.
  • Quotes approved and anonymized before public use.
  • Contradicting evidence captured.
  • Findings labelled by participant type and evidence strength.
  • Raw notes stored securely and excluded from the public portfolio.

13. Participant invitation template

Suggested message: “Hello. I am creating an independent product-management case study about how pharmacy customers and store teams handle bills, membership, savings and follow-up after an in-store purchase. I would value a 30–40 minute conversation about your experience. This is not official Medkart research or a sales call. I will not ask for prescription details or unnecessary personal information, and any portfolio findings will be anonymized. Participation is voluntary.”
